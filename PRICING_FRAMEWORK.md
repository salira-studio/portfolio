# SaaS Pricing Framework — For Common People

A practical guide for presenting software pricing to clients, based on analysis of 5,000+ SaaS products in 2026.

---

## The Short Version

| What to Do | Why |
|------------|-----|
| Use 3 tiers (Starter / Pro / Business) | 43% of all SaaS uses exactly this — buyers expect it |
| Start pricing at ₹499–₹1,499/mo or $10–$25/mo | 60% of tools start under $25/mo — this is where the market lives |
| Offer monthly + annual billing | Monthly for acquisition, annual for retention |
| Give 15–20% annual discount | Industry standard — "2 months free" is easy to explain |
| Offer 14-day free trial | 41% of SaaS use 14 days — it forces a decision |
| Keep it simple | Complex pricing = lost customers |

## The Three-Tier Structure

This is what works. Don't overthink it.

### Tier 1: Starter / Basic
**Who it's for:** Individuals, small businesses, people just starting out
**Price range:** ₹499–₹999/mo ($10–$15/mo)

What to include:
- Core functionality (what the product actually does)
- Limited usage (storage, users, projects — pick what fits)
- Email support only
- Basic integrations

### Tier 2: Pro / Standard (Recommended)
**Who it's for:** Growing businesses, teams, serious users
**Price range:** ₹1,499–₹2,999/mo ($20–$40/mo)

What to include:
- Everything in Starter
- Higher limits (more storage, more users, more projects)
- Priority support
- Advanced features (analytics, automations, integrations)
- This is your "recommended" tier — mark it visually

### Tier 3: Business / Premium
**Who it's for:** Established businesses, larger teams, enterprises
**Price range:** ₹4,999–₹9,999/mo ($60–$100/mo)

What to include:
- Everything in Pro
- Unlimited or very high limits
- SSO, advanced security, audit logs
- Dedicated support
- Custom integrations / API access

## How to Present Pricing to Clients

### Step 1: Understand Their Needs First
Before showing pricing, ask:
- How many people will use this?
- What features do they actually need?
- What's their budget range?
- Are they okay with monthly or prefer annual?

### Step 2: Show the Right Tier
Don't show all three tiers at once unless they ask. Start with the tier that fits their needs. Let them upgrade if they want more.

### Step 3: Explain Value, Not Features

❌ Bad: "You get 50GB storage and 10 user accounts"
✅ Good: "This covers your team of 8 with room to grow — no surprises"

❌ Bad: "Pro includes advanced analytics and custom workflows"
✅ Good: "You'll see exactly where your money goes and automate the repetitive stuff"

### Step 4: Offer Annual Savings
Say this:
> "If you go monthly, it's ₹1,999/month. If you commit for a year, it's ₹16,999 total — that's ₹1,416/month, saving you about ₹7,000 a year."

That's a 17% discount. Easy to explain: "basically 2 months free."

## Pricing Math (Simple Version)

### For Subscription Products
1. **Find the value**: What does your product save or earn the customer?
2. **Price at 10–20% of that value**: If you save them ₹10,000/month, charge ₹1,000–₹2,000/month
3. **Floor**: Never go below ₹499/mo ($10) — it's not worth the support cost
4. **Ceiling**: If you're above ₹5,000/mo ($60), you need a sales team

### For One-Time Projects
1. **Estimate your hours**: How long will this take?
2. **Multiply by your rate**: Your hourly rate × hours = base price
3. **Add 20–30% buffer**: Things always take longer
4. **Present as fixed price**: Clients prefer certainty over hourly guessing

## Discount Rules

| Discount Type | When to Use | Amount |
|---------------|-------------|--------|
| Annual billing | Always offer | 15–20% off monthly |
| First-time discount | New customers hesitant to commit | 10–15% first month |
| Volume discount | 10+ seats or large contracts | 10–25% |
| Loyalty discount | Existing customers renewing | 5–10% |
| Promotional | Black Friday, launch, seasonal | 20–30% (limited time) |

**Rules:**
- Never discount more than 30% — it devalues your product
- Always tie discounts to commitment (annual, multi-year, volume)
- Never use permanent discounts — they train customers to wait
- Limit promos to 1–4 weeks

## Free Tier vs Free Trial

| Option | Best For | Trade-off |
|--------|----------|-----------|
| Free tier (forever) | Products where value grows with usage | Lower barrier, but users may never convert |
| Free trial (14 days) | Products with clear immediate value | Forces decision, but higher barrier |
| Both | Products with a simple use case + a complex one | More work to design, but covers all bases |

**The data says:**
- 51% of SaaS offer a free tier
- 54% offer a free trial
- At ₹1,000/mo ($15) range, free-to-start is almost mandatory
- Above ₹5,000/mo ($60), sales calls make more sense than free trials

## How to Talk About Price

### Do's
- Lead with the value they get, not the cost
- Use concrete numbers: "You'll save ₹X per month"
- Make annual the default (pre-select it)
- Show the savings clearly: "Save ₹7,000/year with annual"
- Offer a payment plan for large projects

### Don'ts
- Don't apologize for the price
- Don't explain your costs (they don't care)
- Don't offer your lowest price first
- Don't say "it depends" — give a range
- Don't hide the price behind "contact sales" unless it's truly enterprise

## Common Mistakes

| Mistake | Why It's Bad | What to Do Instead |
|---------|--------------|-------------------|
| Too many tiers (5+) | Decision paralysis | Stick to 3, max 4 |
| Pricing too low | Attracts wrong customers, can't sustain support | Price at 10–20% of value delivered |
| No annual option | Missed retention + cash flow | Always offer annual with 15–20% discount |
| Hidden fees | Destroys trust | All-in pricing, no surprises |
| Per-user pricing for everything | Punishes growth | Use flat-rate unless user count = value |
| No free tier or trial | 60% of market offers one — you look expensive by comparison | At minimum, offer a 14-day trial |
